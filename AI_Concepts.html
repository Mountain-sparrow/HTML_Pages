1) Big‑Picture Map

AI, ML, DL, LLM, Generative Models — how they fit together

Think of this as a family tree. Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the whole field. Machine Learning (ML) is the part of AI that learns from data. Deep Learning (DL) is a subset of ML that uses neural networks with many layers. Large Language Models (LLMs) are a specific kind of deep learning model trained on huge text corpora. Generative models are models that can create new data (text, images, audio, code) rather than just classify it.

2) How ML Learns

Supervised vs. Unsupervised Learning — labels or no labels

Supervised learning uses labeled examples (“this is spam”, “this is not”). Unsupervised learning has no labels — it discovers structure by itself (clusters, anomalies, patterns). Most real systems mix both.

3) Two Personalities of AI

Generative vs. Discriminative — creator vs. judge

Discriminative models focus on labeling or deciding (“Is this fraud?”). Generative models learn to create data that looks real (new images, text, audio). This section lets you switch perspectives and see how the goals change.

5) Inside the Engines

A peek into generative models

Generative models come in different flavors. Each makes new data in its own way. Use the tabs to explore the core idea behind each one.

Autoregressive models (like many LLMs)

They predict the next piece of data given the previous pieces. Think of writing a sentence one word at a time.

Toy demo: each step chooses the most likely next token.

GANs (Generator + Discriminator)

The generator creates fakes. The discriminator tries to spot fakes. Over time, the generator gets better.

Toy demo: the bars show generator quality vs. discriminator strictness.

Diffusion models

They learn to remove noise from data. Generation starts with noise and gradually cleans it into an image.

Move the slider: left is noisy, right is clean.

VAEs (Variational Autoencoders)

They compress data into a small “latent space” and then decode it back. By moving in that space you can create new variations.

Toy demo: moving the dot changes the reconstructed output.

6) Inside an LLM

Deconstructing how large language models behave

LLMs are giant next‑token predictors. They split text into tokens, map tokens into vectors (embeddings), use attention to mix context, and then pick the next token based on probabilities. This demo is a simplified, visual explanation — not a real model.

Tokenization (toy demo)

Real tokenizers are more complex (they split into sub‑words). This gives the intuition.

Attention heatmap (toy demo)

Darker squares mean a token is “paying attention” to another token.

Next‑token probabilities (toy demo)

Real models compute probabilities over huge vocabularies, then sample or pick the top token.

What LLMs are good and bad at

Great at: summarizing, drafting, translating
Risky at: math without tools, exact facts, timing
Needs help: citations, fresh data, sensitive decisions
Better with: clear prompts + constraints + examples

9) Put It All Together

One‑screen summary

Quick recap

  • AI is the umbrella; ML learns from data; DL uses deep neural nets.
  • LLMs are DL models trained on massive text.
  • Generative models create new data; discriminative models classify or decide.
  • Supervised = labeled data. Unsupervised = patterns without labels.
  • Real value comes from pairing AI with business goals, clean data, and humans in the loop.

When AI is worth it

AI shines when tasks are repetitive, data‑rich, and expensive for humans to do at scale.
